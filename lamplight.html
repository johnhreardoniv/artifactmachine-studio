What it does

  • Tonight picks one game to return to and one to try, and says why.
  • Time adds up your hours by type of game, for your whole library and for each week from the day you start it. Click a type to see each game in it and how it was classified.
  • Insights shows how your taste is moving, what you leave early, what you buy and don't start, and the studios you keep returning to.
  • Next up ranks your backlog, wishlist and other Steam games by how you've actually spent your time, and names the tags and games behind each pick. No AI model is involved.
  • Revisit finds the games you put serious hours into and stopped, and the ones you barely tried.
  • News and Releases follow the games you own and the ones you're waiting for.
  • Connections connects Steam, GOG Galaxy, Epic and Playnite from one page, with numbered setup steps for each.
  • Friends pairs you with a friend who runs Lamplight, to see what you both play and what to play together. Each pairing is just the two of you. Off until you set it up.

What it reads

Lamplight runs on your PC and keeps your data there, with no account and nothing to sign up for. It reads the files Steam keeps on your computer. It also asks Steam's public store and news services about your games, so Steam sees those requests. It can't change anything in Steam, and it can't log in to your account.

The one other service is the Friends relay, and only once you set Friends up. Your games, hours and name are sealed on your PC before they go there, so the relay holds bytes it can't read. Invite codes work once and expire after 7 days unused.

If you use other stores, it also reads what the Epic launcher and GOG Galaxy keep on your PC, and a file you export from Playnite. Either can also bring in Xbox, Battle.net, Ubisoft and others: GOG Galaxy for stores you've linked inside it, Playnite for its library add-ons. It never signs in to them, and it looks each title up on Steam to match it. Those games join your recommendations, and their hours stay out of your Steam time. GOG Galaxy's file has been read on one real PC; the Epic launcher's files and Playnite's export only on sample files.

A free Steam Web API key is optional. It adds achievements and any games missing from those local files.

Start it

  1. Unzip the download somewhere permanent, such as your Documents folder. You'll need Steam installed and 64-bit Windows.
  2. Double-click Lamplight.exe. Your browser opens the app, and a small window stays open while it runs.
  3. To have it start quietly each time you sign in, open a Command Prompt in that folder and run Lamplight.exe autostart on. Weekly hours only count while it's running.

Why Windows warns you

This program isn't code-signed, so Windows may show "Windows protected your PC". If you trust where you got the link, choose "More info", then "Run anyway". To confirm your copy is the published one, run certutil -hashfile lamplight-0.8.0-windows-x64.zip SHA256 and compare the result with this:

35e17b36533c2ad6a566e97a92ba532326b983adec9c9f23fcf59539d81f8339

Mac and Linux

The Mac and Linux download (0.4 MB) runs on your own copy of Node.js, version 22.13 or newer. Unpack it somewhere permanent and run node lamplight.cjs in that folder.

It hasn't been tried on a Mac yet. On Linux it has only been tried in a container with sample Steam files, not with Steam itself. On both, Lamplight can't see which game is running, so your hours update after you stop playing and Steam saves them, with no live sessions. Starting it at sign-in is up to you, and the README shows the command.

To confirm your copy, run sha256sum on Linux or shasum -a 256 on a Mac, and compare the result with this:

Release history

The buttons at the top of this page always download the newest version. To update, download it and replace the old program. Your data stays where it is.

Lamplight was called Playledger until version 0.6.0. If you had Playledger, end it in Task Manager, then start Lamplight: it moves your games, hours and settings over by itself, and takes over autostart if you had it on.
