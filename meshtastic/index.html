LESSON 01 / FOUNDATIONS

What are LoRa and Meshtastic?

LoRa is the radio method. Meshtastic is the messaging system built on top of it.

Two layers, one useful network

LoRa encodes small packets into radio signals using changing-frequency “chirps.” It can hear weak signals, but it trades speed for reach. It is suited to short texts, locations, and telemetry, not calls or large files.

Meshtastic supplies the rules for addressing, channels, encryption, and relaying packets. Your phone is usually just the local screen and keyboard: it connects to your P1 Pro over Bluetooth. The P1 Pro then speaks by LoRa to other nodes. That radio link works without cell service or Internet.

LoRaWAN is different. It also uses LoRa radios, but normally connects end devices through gateways and network servers. A Meshtastic node cannot simply join a LoRaWAN gateway.

Meet your P1 Pro.

A ready-to-use outdoor node with solar power, built-in batteries, GPS, Bluetooth, and a LoRa radio.

Seeed lists a XIAO nRF52840 Plus controller, Wio-SX1262 LoRa module, XIAO L76K positioning module, four installed 18650 batteries, and an external LoRa antenna. The radio arrives with Meshtastic installed. Its battery and GPS distinguish P1 Pro from the base P1.

Your first setup, on a table

  1. Attach the supplied LoRa antenna to the correct antenna connection before powering the radio. Do not transmit with the antenna disconnected.
  2. Activate the node by connecting the supplied USB-C cable to a normal 5 V source. Seeed says the blue mesh light blinks when the device is on; charge it first if it does not respond. Avoid a fast charger.
  3. Install the official Meshtastic app on your phone and pair over Bluetooth. Seeed documents 123456 as the factory pairing code; follow the prompt on your device if it differs.
  4. Set the LoRa region for where you will actually transmit. Then record the displayed firmware, modem preset, frequency slot, and role. Leave the role at CLIENT while learning.
No flash needed to start. This unit is pre-flashed. If you later update it, use Seeed’s exact device instructions; Seeed specifically warns against the NRF-OTA route for this model.

LESSON 03 / COMPATIBILITY

Find a mesh.

A working radio can sound silent when nearby nodes use different settings.

The three radio settings

Two nodes must agree on region, modem preset, and frequency slot before they can decode each other. A preset bundles speed and sensitivity choices. A frequency slot selects a specific frequency inside the regional band.

01RegionWhich legal band?
02Modem presetWhich radio signal shape?
03Frequency slotWhich exact slice of the band?

A messaging channel is a separate concept: it determines which conversation and key a packet belongs to. Secondary channels share the same radio settings. Changing the primary channel’s name can change the automatic frequency slot, so keep it stable during discovery.

Check what your device shows. Meshtastic’s newer US setup paths can produce different initial presets, including Long Fast and Long Turbo. Your firmware and app route matter more than anyone’s remembered “default.”

Try it with one node

With your three radios, use the shared-settings worksheet to compare them side by side.

Record your region, preset, and slot. Listen in a clear location. If your app offers Local Mesh Discovery, try it. A quiet scan can mean no compatible nearby node; it cannot find every private or custom-slot mesh. A local Meshtastic group can tell you what settings people use nearby.

LESSON 04 / THE MESH

Messages can take a detour.

Other nodes can relay a packet when the destination is beyond your direct radio reach.

Asender
⇄
Brelay
⇄
Creceiver

A can hear B; B can hear C. B may rebroadcast A’s packet so C can receive it. Meshtastic uses managed flooding and duplicate suppression rather than guaranteeing an end-to-end connection. The usual hop limit is 3; raising it casually adds traffic to a shared radio channel.

Broadcast or direct?

A channel broadcast is for everyone who can receive and decrypt that channel. A direct message targets one node and uses that node’s public key, usually learned through its NodeInfo announcement. Another node can relay a packet even if it cannot read a private channel’s contents.

Read status labels carefully. A broadcast relay acknowledgment shows that at least one node repeated the packet. It does not prove every intended person received it. A direct-message acknowledgment can come from the destination.

Try it

On a local mesh, send a short ordinary public message and note its status. With a second node, send a message in both directions, then compare broadcast and direct-message status. Leave the hop limit at 3 while learning.

Sources: Mesh algorithm · Encryption · Message statuses

DELIVERY ≠ RELAY

Someone rebroadcasting a packet is evidence of a relay, not proof the recipient saw it.

LESSON 05 / PRIVACY

Decide what you share.

The P1 Pro has GPS. Whether its position is broadcast is a separate setting and your choice.

The public channel is public

Meshtastic’s default public channel uses the published key AQ==. Its packets are encrypted, but anyone with that known key can read them. Write as if speaking in a public place. For a private group, create a secondary channel with a random shared key and give the settings only to the people you intend.

Private traffic still exposes some packet headers so relays can route it. Meshtastic also lacks forward secrecy: a person who records packets and later gets the channel key may be able to read the old traffic. Verify a direct-message contact’s identity before sending sensitive details.

GPS is not the same as sharing GPS

The P1 Pro includes a GNSS receiver, but you can choose where location goes. Each messaging channel has a position-precision setting: 0 sends no position on that channel; 32 is full precision. Review position settings on the public primary channel before mounting the device at home. MQTT and public-map features are additional optional paths to inspect before enabling.

One-node privacy check: Open the primary channel, position, and MQTT settings. Record what is enabled. If you do not want your site shown to nearby users, keep position off the public channel.

Sources: Meshtastic encryption · Channel and position precision · MQTT settings

LESSON 06 / FIELDWORK

Put the sun and terrain to work.

Treat Seeed’s 8–9 km open-area figure as a claim to test, not a promise for your location.

Mount after you have a working link

First confirm pairing, settings, and short-range messaging on the ground. Then choose a stable site with sun exposure and a clear radio view. The supplied bracket lets you angle the panel; Seeed says to mount it diagonally rather than flat so water does not collect. Secure the enclosure and antenna connection following the official installation guide.

The P1 Pro’s four batteries buffer time without sunlight, but runtime depends on messages, GPS activity, temperature, season, and shade. Seeed lists a 0–50 °C charging range; cold weather can stop charging even while the node is operating. Watch battery level over days rather than assuming a “set and forget” installation.

Antenna placement often beats configuration changes. Height and a clear path can matter more than raising the hop limit. Keep the supplied antenna connected before power and protect the cable/connection as the manufacturer directs.

Two-node range test

  1. Match region, preset, slot, and channel. Confirm two-way messages nearby.
  2. Keep one node fixed. Move the other in stages; send short messages both ways.
  3. Log location, approximate distance, direction, hop count, and signal readings. Move the fixed node higher or clearer and repeat a weak spot.

The optional Meshtastic Range Test module can send numbered packets, but it adds traffic; turn it off afterward. The Site Planner can estimate terrain effects, while a real test captures buildings, trees, and local interference the model may miss.

LESSON 07 / NEXT STEPS

Know the edges of the map.

The mesh works offline. Optional Internet bridges and special modules solve different problems.

Extras, explained simply

MQTT

An optional Internet bridge between radio meshes. Nearby Meshtastic radio messaging does not need it. The P1 Pro has no built-in Wi-Fi; an Internet-connected client or gateway is needed for a bridge.

Store & Forward

A separate server feature on compatible hardware that can return some stored text history. Your P1 Pro does not automatically become a guaranteed offline mailbox.

Amateur mode

A separate, licensed operating path that sends traffic unencrypted. Do not enable it simply to seek more power.

Operate in your actual region

Select the Meshtastic region for the country where the radio transmits. In the US, the US region uses 902–928 MHz. US Part 15 devices must accept interference and must not cause harmful interference. Other places have different bands and rules.

Plan for failed links. Meshtastic is a useful supplementary communication tool, but a delivery path depends on compatible settings, reachable nodes, radio conditions, and power. Test the actual route and people before relying on it.

Graduation exercise

Draw your setup: phone → P1 Pro → nearby node → destination. Label each link Bluetooth, direct LoRa, relayed LoRa, or optional Internet. Circle the link most likely to fail, then plan one real-world test.
