Playledger, a ledger of your Steam games

See where your hours go, what to play next, which games you've forgotten, and what's new for the games you own.

Download for Windows (34 MB) Try it with sample data

What it does

  • Tonight picks one game to return to and one to try, and says why.
  • Time adds up your hours by type of game, for your whole library and for each week from the day you start it. Click a type to see each game in it and how it was classified.
  • Insights shows how your taste is moving, what you leave early, what you buy and don't start, and the studios you keep returning to.
  • Next up ranks your backlog, wishlist and other Steam games by how you've actually spent your time, and names the tags and games behind each pick. No AI model is involved.
  • Revisit finds the games you put serious hours into and stopped, and the ones you barely tried.
  • News and Releases follow the games you own and the ones you're waiting for.

What it reads

Playledger runs on your PC and keeps your data there, with no account and nothing to sign up for. It reads the files Steam keeps on your computer. It also asks Steam's public store and news services about your games, so Steam sees those requests. It talks to no other service, it can't change anything in Steam, and it can't log in to your account.

If you use other stores, it also reads what the Epic launcher and GOG Galaxy keep on your PC, and a file you export from Playnite. It never signs in to them, and it looks each title up on Steam to match it. Those games join your recommendations, and their hours stay out of your Steam time. This part has only been tested on sample files, not a real Epic or GOG library.

A free Steam Web API key is optional. It adds achievements and any games missing from those local files.

Start it

  1. Unzip the download somewhere permanent, such as your Documents folder. You'll need Steam installed and 64-bit Windows.
  2. Double-click Playledger.exe. Your browser opens the app, and a small window stays open while it runs.
  3. To have it start quietly each time you sign in, open a Command Prompt in that folder and run Playledger.exe autostart on. Weekly hours only count while it's running.

Why Windows warns you

This program isn't code-signed, so Windows may show "Windows protected your PC". If you trust where you got the link, choose "More info", then "Run anyway". To confirm your copy is the published one, run certutil -hashfile playledger-0.2.0-windows-x64.zip SHA256 and compare the result with this:

7fb89ba41790758b51fccc69284008a9f0b78da40e1a5c082f280d39ff099e90

Mac and Linux

The Mac and Linux download (0.4 MB) runs on your own copy of Node.js, version 22.13 or newer. Unpack it somewhere permanent and run node playledger.cjs in that folder.

It hasn't been tried on a Mac yet. On Linux it has only been tried in a container with sample Steam files, not with Steam itself. On both, Playledger can't see which game is running, so your hours update after you stop playing and Steam saves them, with no live sessions. Starting it at sign-in is up to you, and the README shows the command.
