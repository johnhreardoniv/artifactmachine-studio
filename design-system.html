2 · Colour

A measurement of 24 logos in the website-audit category returned blue 11, neutral 4, orange 3, green 2 — the category is ~46% blue and thick with mascots and gradients. Warm ink-and-paper with one burnt-orange accent is chosen because it is the open lane, not because it is pretty.

5 · Motion

Short and mechanical. Nothing bounces — a machine does not bounce. Anything over 240ms starts to feel like a website performing for you. All of it is disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

6 · Forbidden

Enforced by review, not by the compiler. Each has a reason; none is arbitrary.

  • Blue as primary — 46% of the category is already blue
  • Animals, mascots, characters — the category is full of them (a frog, a bear, a cat, a dog, a lighthouse)
  • Circles and roundels as containers — soft, consumer, generic
  • Gradients as decoration — gradient blobs date a page instantly
  • Gauges, speedometers, dials — every performance tool already has one
  • Globes, magnifying glasses — the most exhausted icons in software
  • Single-letter monograms — "A" collides with everything
  • Rounded corners beyond 3px — except the 10px chamfer, which is a cut
  • Colour as the only signal — always pair with text or shape
  • The word "Artifact" alone — a live Class 42 registration is reported on the bare term. Always both words.
  • "AI-made" as a selling point — in 2026 that is a liability signal on a paid tool, not a differentiator
  • "Every tool runs on Apify" — never state platform dependence where identity belongs
